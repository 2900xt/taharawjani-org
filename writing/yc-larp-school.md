A fully-paid trip to SF, the startup capital of the world. YC rented the chase center, invited cracked speakers (Jensen Huang, Sam Altman, Alexandr Wang, etc), and paid millions of dollars in california taxes to cover food and travel

## YC

My take: YC is now the new “investment banking/consulting” for ambitious college students who don’t know what to do with their life.

And the math makes sense too. YC takes ~200 companies a batch. That’s ~500 people a batch. x4 that you get ~2000 people a year. If you’re doing CS at HMS, you’re essentially guaranteed a spot at YC. And that’s just YC—there are many pre-seed startup accelerators, such as Speedrun, HF0, and even smaller ones like f.inc. Truly, it’s NEVER been easier to start a startup.

That’s really noble, tbh. Making a startup is waay cooler than banking or consulting, but there used to be a key differentiator. Startups were for people who didn’t really care about the money, who wanted to build, to make something people want, not chase prestiege and raise round after round, hating their life. The YC brand becoming “prestegious” recently invited these bunch into the startup world, which is (imo), the main reason we have so many slop startups and LARP companies.

## Culture

I really admire bay area culture. As an outsider, I lowkey thought it would be extremely toxic and hypercompetitive, and it is, to some extent. However, people here really do push you to become the best version of yourself (or LARP it). Idk. If you’re at a top school in the bay, and you’re pretty chill, the amount of cracked people you interact with in a day-to-day basis is genuinely insane.

That’s also why Berkeley and Stanford are so crazy for startups. They’re genuinely a 30-minute uber away from downtown SF. If you’re dropping out of those schools, you keep your network, clubs, and can always go visit friends on weekends, making building much less isolating and more accessible.

The best part was meeting really fricking cool people. From all parts of the world. International kids. Cracked but thoughtful rising freshmen at top schools. Funny af people that I larped going to Tel Aviv University (#8 in unicorn founders btw) with.

However, it kinda saddened me to see a lot of the speakers, partners, and higher ups in SF distancing themselves from the culture and problems that their own city face. I was talking to one very rich person, and they said: “Uber drivers shouldn’t exist because it’s a low-skill job.” Another narrative was: “Homeless people should just work harder.” You can deadass see the permenant underclass developing in Tenderloin LMAO. You can’t use the terms “NGMI” and “GMI” unless you’ve visited SF imao.

Overall, the trip was 1000% worth, and it made me realize that I was probably going to be staying in school for a while, possibly until figuring out what exactly I want to go into. Startups shouldn’t be something we larp. We should truly have a vision that you have an insane amount of belief in, to the point where you can go all-in.
